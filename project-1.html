Project 1 — Median Household Income and its Association with Lava Flow Hazard in Hawaii

GEOG 395 (Cartography and Visualization) · May 2026 · ArcGIS Pro, ArcGIS Online, ArcGIS StoryMaps

This project covers lava flow hazard in Hawaii County, Hawaii, and outputs maps displaying the relationship between risk and income. It attempts to discover a relationship between lower median household income in Hawaii and susceptibility to lava hazards.

The problem

The question is: Is there a correlation between communities who are susceptible to a larger risk of lava flow and their income?

My role

This project was individually completed.

Data and method

Data

  • Hawaii Lava Flow Hazard Zones, Hawaii Emergency Shelters, and Global Volcanoes datasets (ArcGIS REST Feature Services)
  • ArcGIS Median Household Income and Population Density layers
  • Media and image sources: Hawaii Volcanoes Of The Big Island, Britannica, Honolulu Civil Beat, and The New Yorker

Method

  1. Data acquisition and preparation
  2. Analyze data to make it fit for study (selecting relevant layers, evaluating data reliability and importance)
  3. Symbolization and validation of data
  4. Publish data and create narrative in ArcGIS StoryMaps

What I found

It was found that households with lower and median incomes tend to be more at risk of lava flow hazard, as they are spatially further away from shelters and escape routes.

  • Median-income households are often located in higher lava flow risk zones.
  • Lower-income communities face greater vulnerability due to increased distance from emergency shelters.
  • Spatial access to primary escape routes correlates with community income levels across Hawaii County.

What I would do differently

Next time, the study area should be expanded to include ALL Hawaiian islands to get a better look at the relationship.
